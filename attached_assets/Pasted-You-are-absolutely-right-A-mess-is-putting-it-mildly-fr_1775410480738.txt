You are absolutely right. "A mess" is putting it mildly—from a data integrity standpoint, this is a disaster.
The UI layout itself is actually quite sleek, but the backend mapping is suffering from major "data vomit." The app is pulling conflicting data from multiple endpoints and displaying it all on the same screen without a single source of truth. If a quant or prop trader saw this, they would immediately close the terminal.
Here are the four fatal data collisions happening in this single scroll:
1. The P/E Paradox
The app cannot agree on Tesla's valuation.
 * Fundamentals Section: Shows a P/E of 333.9.
 * Valuation Section (right below it): Shows a Trailing P/E of 29.9x.
 * The Problem: These are lightyears apart. Furthermore, the "Earnings Yield" is listed at 5.26%, which implies a P/E of roughly 19x. None of the valuation metrics are mathematically synced.
2. The Phantom Shares
 * Fundamentals Section: Lists Shares at 3.75B. (This checks out math-wise: $1.35T Market Cap / $360.59 Price = ~3.74B shares).
 * Share Structure Section: Lists Shares Outstanding at 7.19B.
 * The Problem: It’s nearly doubling the actual float in one section, which ruins any per-share metric calculations derived from that specific endpoint.
3. The Broken "Upside" Math
Look at the Earnings & Analyst section:
 * Current Price: $360.59
 * Mean Price Target: $288.98
 * Upside to Target: +7.4% (in Green)
 * The Problem: A target of $288.98 from a price of $360.59 is roughly a -20% downside risk, yet the app is blindly coloring it green and calculating a positive upside. This is actively dangerous for a trader.
4. Margin Schizophrenia
You have two completely different sections displaying margins with entirely different numbers.
 * Profitability Section: Gross Margin 33.3% | Op Margin 20.5% | Net Margin 14.5%
 * Margins Section (Bottom): Gross Margin 49.2% | Op Margin 21.3% | Net Margin 12.3%
 * The Problem: You are likely pulling TTM (Trailing Twelve Months) in one section and MRQ (Most Recent Quarter) in the other, but failing to label them, making the data look broken.
The Verdict & The Fix
This happens when a frontend is rendering data from multiple different API calls (e.g., mixing Finnhub for estimates, Polygon for price, and standard SEC filings for fundamentals) without a reconciliation layer.
To fix this:
 * Enforce a Single Source of Truth: If Market Cap is calculated via Price * Shares, define which share count endpoint the entire app must use.
 * Deduplicate: Delete the bottom "Margins" section entirely. It’s redundant and conflicting. Keep the "Profitability" one and label it clearly as TTM or MRQ.
 * Sanity Check the Math: Write a frontend script that checks: IF (Price Target < Current Price) -> Color = Red, Label = "Downside".
