This 130‑name universe is exactly what you want for options as a *business*: concentrated, ultra‑liquid, and structurally aligned with defined‑risk spreads and PDT constraints.[1][2][3]

## Universe quality

- Your list matches where most of the equity options volume actually lives: SPY, QQQ, IWM, TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, AMZN, AMD, META, MSFT, COIN, etc., which are consistently among the most active options underlyings.[2][4][1]
- You’ve kept broad sector coverage (tech, financials, healthcare, energy, consumer, industrials) while still hugging higher‑cap, higher‑flow names where fills and spreads are reliably good.[4][5][2]
- Including a few levered/index proxies (TQQQ, SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, GLD, SLV, USO) gives you “macro knobs” to express views without chasing junk tickers.[3][1]



## Alignment with your constraints

- For defined‑risk verticals and credit spreads, tight spreads + deep open interest are more important than raw “edge,” and this list focuses exactly on those underlyings.[6][7][8][9][2]
- SPY/QQQ being explicitly *forgiven* is critical: their options are among the deepest and most actively traded globally, and they are ideal for 7–45 DTE spreads that don’t chew up too many day trades.[10][1][2][3]
- Cutting thin names directly attacks your historical pain points: slippage, “fake” flow in illiquid names, and FOMO entries in stuff with trash microstructure.[8][9][11][2][6]



## Architecture / workflow fit

- For an automated Deterministic Scanner + AI Strategist, hard‑locking to a single liquid universe is huge: every layer (scanner, strategist, AI) debates only in symbols where execution quality is high and historical data is dense.[7][12][13]
- Using the broader 383 only for macro/breadth (Market Pulse) while routing *all* trade idea generation through the 130 keeps regime detection broad but trade selection narrow and professional.[5][12][13]
- This also simplifies backfilling, factor calculations, and Monte Carlo/what‑if because you’re not wasting compute on illiquid tails.[12][13][3]



## Minor refinements I’d consider

- Periodically revalidate membership (maybe quarterly) against an options‑volume ranking, so if a name permanently falls off in activity you can swap it for a rising star without bloating the list.[14][2][4]
- Within each sector, tag a few “prime” underlyings for day‑trading vs swing spreads (e.g., TSLA/NVDA/COIN for faster intraday, JNJ/UNH/XOM/COST for steadier swing structures).[15][16][1][2]
- Keep a separate, *tiny* “experimental” list (5–10 tickers) for anything new you’re tempted to trade, and only promote to the core 130 if it proves itself on volume and spreads over time.[17][2][7][8]
