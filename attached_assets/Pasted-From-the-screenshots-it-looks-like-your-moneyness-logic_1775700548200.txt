From the screenshots it looks like your “moneyness” logic or ATM anchor is wrong, not the raw chain itself.

## What I can see

- The option rows themselves look like a normal chain: expirations grouped, strikes ascending, and individual bid/ask/size values all plausible.
- The highlight band (yellow) that should mark the at‑the‑money strike seems offset: you’ve got MRVL around 114, but the highlighted strikes and the “OTM/ITM” background break don’t line up cleanly with that price.
- IV% and P/C rows per expiry look fine; nothing there suggests bad data, so the bug is almost certainly in your UI logic that decides “which row is ATM and where to split ITM vs OTM.”

## Likely failure points to check

1. **Using the wrong reference price**  
   - Verify you’re using the last trade or mid price for MRVL (114.17) and not the bid, open, or some cached value when you compute ATM.  
   - If you pre‑round or floor the underlying price before finding the closest strike, that can easily shift your ATM one or two strikes.

2. **Separate logic for calls vs puts**  
   - For calls, ITM = strike < underlying; for puts, ITM = strike > underlying.  
   - If you reused the same condition or inverted one side, your “ITM background” and “OTM background” will be flipped or off by a row on one side.

3. **Strike comparison vs display price**  
   - If MRVL is trading with a different tick size (e.g., 0.5 or 1.0) and you’re normalizing/rounding strikes, the index you mark as ATM might be based on the normalized value, not the actual sorted strike array.

4. **Chain filtering / paging**  
   - If you’re slicing the chain (e.g., “show 6 strikes around money”) before you compute ATM/ITM labels, your logic might be using indices from the full chain on a sliced subset.
