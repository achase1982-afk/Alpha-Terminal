Here is the exact blueprint for your two-step UI flow, specifically tailored for the Jobs Report (NFP) to give institutional and quant traders exactly what they need without wasting screen space.
Step 1: The Summary Card (The Quick Glance)
This small box replaces your current text paragraph. It must be instantly scannable, color-coded, and devoid of conversational text. It should only contain the highest-impact headline number and its immediate context.
• Header: Event Name and Release Time (e.g., "Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) | 8:30 AM ET").
• The Grid (3 columns):
• Actual: The released number (e.g., 265K).
• Estimate: The market consensus (e.g., 240K).
• Previous: The prior month's initial number (e.g., 275K).
• Visual Indicators: The "Actual" number must be color-coded. If Actual > Estimate, color it Green. If Actual < Estimate, color it Red.
• Action Button: A small, clearly visible "Read More" button in the bottom corner to trigger the slide-up sheet.
Step 2: The Bottom Sheet (The Full Single-Page Breakdown)
When the user clicks "Read More," this sheet slides up. It should be highly dense, require zero scrolling, and group the data into logical macroeconomic buckets.
Section 1: The "Big 3" Core Metrics
Display these in a clean, multi-row table showing Actual, Estimate, and Previous for each:
• Headline NFP: Total jobs added/lost.
• Unemployment Rate (U-3): The percentage of the labor force actively seeking work.
• Average Hourly Earnings (MoM & YoY): The wage growth metric, which is critical for inflation modeling.
Section 2: Prior Month Revisions
This is often more important to quants than the headline number itself.
• Net Revisions: Display the combined total revisions for the previous two months (e.g., "2-Month Net Revision: -45K"). Color-code this red if negative and green if positive.
Section 3: Secondary Labor Metrics
Place these side-by-side in a compact row to provide context to the unemployment rate:
• Labor Force Participation Rate: The percentage of the working-age population employed or seeking work.
• Average Weekly Hours: A leading indicator for labor demand.
Section 4: Key Sector Breakdown
Quants look here to see exactly where the jobs are coming from to adjust their equity portfolios. Keep it to the most market-moving sectors:
• Total Private Payrolls (vs. Government jobs).
• Manufacturing Payrolls.
• Leisure & Hospitality.
Section 5: The Source Link
• Action Button: A prominent "Read Full Report" button anchored at the top right or bottom right of the sheet, linking directly to the raw Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) release.
